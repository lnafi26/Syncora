Before the first cut

The hardest part can happen before the timeline even opens.

Finding music, second-guessing the choice, then scrubbing through a track looking for useful moments can quietly eat a huge chunk of editing time.

01

Describe the direction.

Start with the edit you actually want to make: mood, pace, structure, vocals, and the visual intention the music needs to support.

02

Reduce the search.

Nova narrows a huge musical space to three ranked tracks, with enough context to understand the fit and enough freedom to disagree.

03

Map the useful moments.

Pulsar turns the chosen recording into an editor-facing Signal so you can see where meaningful musical changes may support visual movement.

Two focused tools

Discover with Nova. Read the track with Pulsar.

Nova

Three choices. Not another endless feed.

Nova turns your creative brief into a focused shortlist, ranks the options, and lets you listen before deciding what actually belongs in your edit.

Pulsar

A musical map built for an editor.

Pulsar finds a useful source window, maps measured transition points, and separates them into Primary, Secondary, and Tertiary layers you can explore.

Guidance, not autopilot

The computer narrows the possibilities. The editor makes the decision.

Syncora is designed to surface context, not replace judgment. Listen to the shortlist. Ignore the first-ranked option if another track feels better. Use a Pulsar cue when it helps, skip it when it does not.
